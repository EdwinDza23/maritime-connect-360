Survey Workflow

From initial setup to
final publication — fully structured.

Every bunker quantity survey follows a consistent, traceable 6-stage operational sequence. No steps skipped. No data lost.

📝
Stage 01
Create Survey OPEN
Nominate fuel grades (supports up to 3 grades) and assign the designated Attend Surveyor to initialize the survey record.
⚓
Stage 02
Field Inspection
Conduct Remaining On-Board (ROB) assessments, complete mandatory safety checklists, and record opening soundings on supplying barge (BTMR) and vessel (VMR).
⏱️
Stage 03
Bunkering Logs
Log time-events, pumping rate pressures, manifold pressures, and track manifolds representative samples throughout custody transfer.
📊
Stage 04
Close soundings
Compare opening and closing sounding readings, applying draft/trim/list corrections and ASTM volume calculations.
⚖️
Stage 05
Out-turn & NOP
Compare out-turn calculations (Vessel received vs. Barge supplied vs. BDN quantity) and instantly generate Statement of Facts (SOF) or official Note of Protest (NOP).
✉️
Stage 06
Admin Publish PUBLISHED
The onshore operations office conducts administrative reviews, publishes the final certified survey report, and automatically emails it to the buyer.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Everything you need to know about BQS. Can't find what you're looking for? Our maritime experts are ready to help.

How does BQS handle surveys across multiple vessels simultaneously?
BQS is designed for multi-vessel operations. The ongoing surveys dashboard shows all active surveys across your entire fleet simultaneously, with real-time status and progress indicators. You can assign different surveyors to different vessels and track everything from one central view — regardless of fleet size.
What types of bunker surveys does BQS support?
BQS supports the full range of bunker quantity surveys — bunkering surveys, on-hire ROB, off-hire ROB, on-delivery, and off-delivery. Surveyors can nominate up to 3 fuel grades per survey. Charter ROB on/off-hire certificates are also generated directly from the survey data.
What does BQS actually measure and calculate?
BQS captures and calculates: Vessel Measurement Report (VMR) — opening and closing soundings in all vessel fuel tanks with ASTM volume/density/temperature corrections; Remaining on Board (ROB) — fuel quantities before and after bunkering; Barge Tank Measurement Report (BTMR) — independent audit of all non-cargo spaces on the supplying barge to detect hidden fuel diversion; and Mass Flow Meter (MFM) — meter ticket quantities where applicable. All calculations follow ASTM tables and are locked upon submission.
How does BQS verify custody transfer and protect against disputes?
BQS cross-references three independent quantity sources: the Vessel figure (VMR), the Barge figure (BTMR), and the Bunker Delivery Note (BDN). Any discrepancy between these three automatically triggers the Notice of Protest (NOP) generator. The NOP includes checkboxes for Cappuccino effect, blending suspicion, and actual shortage — and is formatted for immediate service on the vessel or barge. A Statement of Facts (SOF) documents the full time-event log. Together these documents form a legally defensible record for P&I club claims or commercial arbitration.
How is the platform's administrative control structured?
Administrators maintain all master directories via the web platform to expedite field entry and ensure data integrity. Through the web dashboard, admins pre-configure clients, vessels, bunker tankers (barges), suppliers, cargo officers, captains, and chief engineers — as well as standardize fuel grade settings and time log activities so every surveyor follows the same structured workflow.
How is report generation and distribution handled?
BQS automatically generates professionally formatted PDF reports from survey data. A preliminary report is available immediately upon field completion. The final certified report is generated when the onshore office reviews and publishes the survey — at which point the survey status changes to PUBLISHED and email distribution is triggered automatically to all configured recipients (buyers, owners, P&I clubs).
How are off-spec fuel and supplier diversion risks detected?
BQS compares the delivered fuel density against the configured benchmark for each grade. A density outside the acceptable deviation threshold triggers an automatic quality alert. Independently, the BTMR audits all non-cargo compartments on the supplying barge — if undisclosed fuel is found in tanks that should be empty, this is flagged as a potential diversion event. Sampling and seal records (port, manifold, and cargo) are also logged with seal numbers to support laboratory analysis if needed.
Is historical survey data migrated from existing systems?
Yes. Our onboarding team provides data migration support to import existing survey records from spreadsheets and other formats into BQS. Historical surveys can be uploaded as PDF documents and linked to vessel records, maintaining your complete survey history in one searchable repository.
